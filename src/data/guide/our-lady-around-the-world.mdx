    Buyers often use "Our Lady of..." as if every devotion worked the same way. In practice, some titles come from
    reported apparitions, some from a venerated statue or icon, some from a religious order or a feast, and some from a

<GuideSection eyebrow="Regional notes" title="What sells where, and why">
  <p>
    <strong>Latin America</strong> is dominated by Guadalupe and Aparecida, with strong local devotions in each country.
    Guadalupe is the reference point for Mexico and has wide reach across the Americas; Aparecida is the central
    devotion in Brazil. Buyers in this region often want the image to match the local shrine closely.
  </p>
  <p class="mt-4">
    <strong>Europe</strong> has a long list of shrine-based devotions, with Lourdes and Fatima the most internationally
    recognised, alongside national devotions such as Czestochowa in Poland and Knock in Ireland. European buyers tend to
    be precise about the title and the feast.
  </p>
  <p class="mt-4">
    <strong>North America</strong> imports the major European and Latin American devotions and also has its own approved
    shrine devotion at Champion, Wisconsin. <strong>Asia and Africa</strong> have significant local devotions, including
    Kibeho in Rwanda and Akita in Japan, and buyers may ask for them alongside the international titles.
  </p>
</GuideSection>

<GuideSection eyebrow="Accuracy" title="How to describe a Marian title without overclaiming" background="cream">
  <p>
    Catholic teaching distinguishes public revelation from private revelation. Approved apparitions can be believed
    piously, but they are not part of the deposit of faith and Catholics are not obliged to accept them. A product
    listing should describe the devotion and its imagery, not present an apparition as a doctrinal requirement.
  </p>
  <p class="mt-5">
    The same care applies to claims about the object itself. Unless it is documented, an item should not be described as
    blessed, consecrated, miracle-working or officially approved. Describing the title, the image and the materials
    accurately is enough, and it is what serious buyers expect.
  </p>
</GuideSection>

<GuideSection eyebrow="Next step" title="Confirm the title, then approve the artwork">
  <p>
    Marian designs are judged on details: the colour of the mantle, the presence of a crown, the grotto, the image on
    the reverse, the spelling of the title. Ask the customer to approve a physical sample or a high-resolution proof
    before bulk production, and keep that approval with the written specification. It is the simplest way to avoid a
    devotional product that looks right in general but wrong to the people who know the devotion.
  </p>
